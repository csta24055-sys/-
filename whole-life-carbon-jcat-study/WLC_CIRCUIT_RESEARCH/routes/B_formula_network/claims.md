## H-B2 — Passive LTI RC cannot equal a kinked or jumping kernel [H] (proof sketch; pending route D; no novelty claimed: corollary of TH-1/TH-7 and textbook network theory [L])
**Sketch.**
1. Take a lumped, linear, time-invariant RC network with independent sources that are real-analytic in T for T>0, such as DC switched on at T=0 or sinusoids.
2. Every node voltage and branch current then has the form y(T) = Σ p_i(T)e^{s_i T}, an exponential polynomial (for passive RC: real s_i ≤ 0). It is real-analytic on (0,∞).
3. Let κ(T) be a kernel with a knot c: a kink of w_k at k or k+1, or a jump of θ_ε at sw(τ). Suppose y = κ on an open interval I with c ∈ I.
4. Then y equals an affine function α₁+β₁T on the part of I left of c. By the identity theorem, y ≡ α₁+β₁T on (0,∞).
5. On the right of c, y = α₂+β₂T with (α₂,β₂) ≠ (α₁,β₁). That is a contradiction for a kink. For a jump, y is continuous and κ is not.

**Limits of the sketch.** It assumes analytic sources. A passive network driven by a step or ramp **source placed at the knot** passes the non-analyticity through. In that case the switching lives in the source (class c), not in the RC network.

**Executed illustration [F]** (`results/hb2_illustration.json`). Least-squares fits by c₀+Σc_i e^{−λ_i T} on [0,2], a superset of passive RC responses, with 1–24 fixed time constants:
- The sup error for the jump stays ≥ 0.501 for every order. This is consistent with the elementary bound J/2 = 0.5 for any continuous function.
- For the kink w₀ the sup error falls from 0.242 to 0.019 but stays > 0 and is largest near the knot.

## H-B3 — Refrigerant: continuous RC vs sample-hold + reset (executed against refcalc)
- **[F]** The S/H automaton built from raw inputs matches refcalc B1-02/B1-03/B1-04 with relative error 0.0 at all points:
  - whole scenario M: 300 T points
  - single derived device without top-up (r = 0.141): 242 T points
  - kernel grid on S and M, including in-band points
- **[F]** RC-b (same S/H resets, continuous RC decay inside a cell, with e^{−1/RC} = q = 1−r):
  - Single device: relative error ≤ 1.1e-15 at whole years and up to **2.88e-3** inside cells. The latter equals (q^{1/2} − (1−r/2))/(1−r/2) at half years.
  - B1-02 leak: up to 5.77e-2 relative, at T = 0.25.
  - Whole M (32 of 40 devices have top-up): B1-04 1.36e-4, B1-02 4.9e-3.
- **[F]** RC-a (a physical RC whose reset switch closes at the event time) has a relative error of up to 2.88e-3 (single device).
- **Verdict: hypothesis confirmed in the tested scope.**
- **[F] Refinement / counterexample.**
  - The S/H needs a **late reset**: a comparator that fires after its own cell has closed (integer τ, because sw(τ) > τ = cell end) must reset the held state and re-initialise the in-cell integrator.
  - Without it the error is up to 3.15e-2 (B1-04) and 3.27e-2 (B1-02) on M.
  - With it the automaton also matches refcalc **inside the band (K, sw(τ)]**. That band is excluded from 𝒟′ because the msp interval form deviates there. We observed the deviation: msp interval vs refcalc 3.12e-2 at T=15.0000000075 in M, and SUM-03(msp) 2.5e-5.
  - This band agreement is [F] on 2 scenarios only and is not claimed beyond them.

## H-B4 — Is the circuit just an isomorphic rewriting? (counts in `results/hb4_counts.json`)
- **[F]** The program sizes are confirmed: P1 1,528 steps (1,358 sp + 170 match), TIME 95 (all sp), AGG 166 (156 sp + 10 match).
- **[F] Memoryless families (atom, cell, gated cell, density).**
  - Under the naive "irreducible parameters after EMIT" metric, the circuit parameter count is lower in every family. Examples:
    - B4-02 M: 5,182 vs 5,394
    - B1-03 M: 329 vs 522
    - year-cell integrator: 259 vs 260
  - Tracing each case shows the reduction comes from:
    1. fan-out of one source to several switches (factorisation a·N(T): the msp form keeps row amplitude × per-row count in P1/TIME before EMIT expands it);
    2. one shared w_k node per year (msp TIME also computes `t.w` once per year);
    3. the implicit origin threshold (the −1).
  - **No circuit-specific compression was found.** H-B4 is not refuted in substance, but the verdict depends on the metric.
- **[F] Recurrent refrigerant family.** The S/H automaton stores O(devices) state, while msp stores O(devices × H):
  - automaton on M: 4-tuple (160 continuous, 81 discrete, 360 thresholds, 471 table entries)
  - msp on M: a 5,200-row device×year table and 10,400 B1-02 knots
  - **This refutes H-B4 in the stored-state metric.**
  - The cost is sequential evaluation: ⌈T⌉−1 boundary updates per query from T=0. Measured: 1.0 ms for 10 T on M, against 38 µs for knot+interval evaluation after the 0.35 s P1 build.
  - This is the standard recurrence-versus-unrolled-table trade-off [L], not a new result.
- **Verdict: PARTIAL.** Refuted for the refrigerant family in stored state; not refuted for the memoryless families once sharing is controlled for. No speed advantage was found for the circuit path.
