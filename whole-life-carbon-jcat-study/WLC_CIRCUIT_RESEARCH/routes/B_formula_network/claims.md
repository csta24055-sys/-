## H-B2 — Lumped LTI RC with analytic sources cannot equal a kinked or jumping kernel [H→ confirmed-as-textbook by D for LTI] (no novelty claimed: corollary of TH-1/TH-7 and textbook network theory [L])
**Scope (D pass 2).** The claim is only about **lumped, linear, time-invariant** RC networks whose independent sources are real-analytic on (0,∞), for example DC switched on at T=0 or sinusoids.
- It is **not** a physical impossibility for passive circuits in general. A time-varying passive network reproduces w_k exactly: an ideal switch, closed only on (k,k+1], connecting a DC current source to a capacitor.
- A nonlinear passive ideal-diode clipper gives clip(·,0,1) exactly, and an ideal switch with a charged capacitor gives jumps.
- The labels "active" (K10) and "class e" (K37) in MAPPING.csv are therefore **relative to the grammar 𝒢 and to the LTI/analytic-source restriction**.

**Sketch.**
1. Take a lumped LTI RC network with independent sources that are real-analytic on (0,∞).
2. Every node voltage and branch current is real-analytic on (0,∞). For exponential-polynomial sources it is the exponential polynomial y(T) = Σ p_i(T)e^{s_i T}, with real s_i ≤ 0 for passive RC; s = 0 includes the ramp c·T of a DC current source into a capacitor. Analyticity alone is enough for the argument.
3. Let κ(T) be a kernel with a knot c: a kink of w_k at k or k+1, or a jump of θ_ε at sw(τ). Suppose y = κ on an open interval I with c ∈ I.
4. Then y equals an affine function α₁+β₁T on the part of I left of c. By the identity theorem, y ≡ α₁+β₁T on (0,∞).
5. On the right of c, y = α₂+β₂T with (α₂,β₂) ≠ (α₁,β₁). That is a contradiction for a kink. For a jump, y is continuous and κ is not.

**Limits.** A step or ramp **source placed at the knot** carries the non-analyticity. The switching then lives in the source (class c).

**Executed illustration [F]** (`results/hb2_illustration.json`, regenerated). Least-squares fits by c₀ + c₁T + Σc_i e^{−λ_i T} on [0,2], with 1–24 fixed λ_i and the ramp term T added after D's review.
- What the family covers: it contains the DC-source LTI RC responses with poles at 0, including the double root, and on the λ grid. It is **not** a superset of all LTI RC responses. The earlier "superset of passive RC responses" wording was false, because the ramp term was missing; it is corrected.
- Jump: the sup error stays ≥ 0.5004 for every order. This is consistent with the elementary bound J/2 = 0.5, which holds for every continuous response.
- Kink w₀: the sup error falls from 0.094 to 0.019 and is > 0 at every finite order. It does tend to 0 as the order grows (Müntz-type density), so only finite-order non-equality is claimed.

## H-B3 — Refrigerant: continuous RC vs sample-hold + reset (executed against refcalc)
- **[F]** The S/H automaton built from raw inputs matches refcalc B1-02/B1-03/B1-04 with relative error 0.0 at all points:
  - whole scenario M: 300 T points
  - single derived device without top-up (r = 0.141): 242 T points
  - kernel grid on S and M, including in-band points
- **[F]** RC-b (same S/H resets, continuous RC decay inside a cell, with e^{−1/RC} = q = 1−r):
  - Single device: relative error ≤ 1.1e-15 at whole years and up to **2.88e-3** inside cells. The latter equals (q^{1/2} − (1−r/2))/(1−r/2) at half years.
  - B1-02 leak: up to 5.77e-2 relative, at T = 0.25.
  - Whole M (32 of 40 devices have top-up): B1-04 1.36e-4, B1-02 4.9e-3.
- **[F]** RC-a (a physical RC whose reset switch closes at the event time) has a relative error of up to 2.88e-3 (single device).
- **Verdict: hypothesis confirmed in the tested scope.**
- **[F] Refinement / counterexample.**
  - The S/H needs a **late reset**: a comparator that fires after its own cell has closed (integer τ, because sw(τ) > τ = cell end) must reset the held state and re-initialise the in-cell integrator.
  - Without it the error is **scenario-dependent**: up to 3.15e-2 (B1-04) / 3.27e-2 (B1-02) on M, and 4.63e-2 / 7.28e-3 on EV3. D saw up to 0.97 on EV6/EV10. A missing reset corrupts every later T.
  - With it the automaton also matches refcalc (0.0) **inside the open band (K, sw(τ))**, where the msp interval form deviates: 3.12e-2 at T=15.0000000075 on M, 2.67e-3 on EV3, and SUM-03(msp) 2.5e-5. D saw up to 1.24.
  - At T = τ(1+1e-9) = sw(τ) the interval form agrees again (≤5.6e-16 on all 4 scenarios). The observed deviating set is therefore the open band (K, sw(τ)) of 03 §5.4. 03 §3.4's closed (K, sw(τ)] is a conservative exclusion from 𝒟′. Neither is a defect of the original theory.
  - This band agreement is [F] on 4 scenarios here (+2 by D) and is not claimed beyond them.

## H-B4 — Is the circuit just an isomorphic rewriting? (counts in `results/hb4_counts.json`)
- **[F]** The program sizes are confirmed: P1 1,528 steps (1,358 sp + 170 match), TIME 95 (all sp), AGG 166 (156 sp + 10 match).
- **[F] Memoryless families (atom, cell, gated cell, density).**
  - Under the naive "irreducible parameters after EMIT" metric, the circuit parameter count is lower in every family. Examples:
    - B4-02 M: 5,182 vs 5,394
    - B1-03 M: 329 vs 522
    - year-cell integrator: 259 vs 260
  - Tracing each case shows the reduction comes from:
    1. fan-out of one source to several switches (factorisation a·N(T): the msp form keeps row amplitude × per-row count in P1/TIME before EMIT expands it);
    2. one shared w_k node per year (msp TIME also computes `t.w` once per year);
    3. the implicit origin threshold (the −1).
  - **No circuit-specific compression was found.** H-B4 is not refuted in substance, but the verdict depends on the metric.
- **[F] Recurrent refrigerant family — corrected after D pass 2: a time–memory trade-off, not a state saving.**
  - My first statement ("stores O(devices) state vs O(devices×H), refutes H-B4 in the stored-state metric") compared the automaton against msp's **unmerged** per-device pieces: a 5,200-row table and 10,400 B1-02 knots. That is a metric artefact. **Withdrawn.**
  - Re-counted by me (`hb4_counts.py`): rampstep.merge + KnotSpectrum, distinct knots, coefficients = 1 + 2·knots.
    - BM-M merged knot forms: B1-02 263 + B1-03 417 + B1-04 383 = **1,063** coefficients. D's canon count is 263 + 417 + 345 = 1,025; my B1-04 jump-form count has 60 instead of 41 event knots, so canon is a further reduction.
    - Automaton on BM-M: tuple sum **1,072**, or 712 distinct numbers if event times are counted once.
    - BM-S: merged 317 vs automaton 242 / 87.
    - The two are the same order: no asymptotic state saving, only a constant factor either way.
  - Random access, 200 random T, median of 5:
    - Automaton replay from T=0 per query: 57.6 ms on M, **343×** slower than the msp knot + interval evaluation (0.17 ms). D measured about 100×.
    - On S: 7.8 ms vs 0.14 ms (56×).
    - One sorted sweep over all 200 T: 2.1 ms (M).
  - This is the standard recurrence-versus-table trade-off [L], not a new result.
- **Verdict: H-B4 not refuted.** No circuit-specific compression was found in any family. Memoryless families are equivalent once source fan-out and w_k sharing are controlled for. The refrigerant automaton trades sequential evaluation for a constant-factor difference in stored numbers. No speed advantage was found.
